# Players, Ratings, and the Simulation

---

Every player in EBL has ratings.

Those ratings influence how players perform during games and how they develop over time.

Here's how the system works. ⚾🧵

---

A player is not defined by a single rating.

Each player has a collection of ratings that describe different baseball skills.

---

Those individual ratings are combined into an overall rating.

The overall rating provides a quick summary of a player's current ability.

---

Players also have an age.

Age affects player development and long-term roster decisions.

---

Hitters have ratings against both right-handed and left-handed pitching.

Contact, plate discipline, gap power, and home run power can all vary depending on the matchup.

---

Pitchers have ratings against both right-handed and left-handed hitters.

Power, control, movement, and pitch quality all contribute to their performance.

---

Ratings are not cosmetic.

They directly affect what happens during games.

---

When a pitch is thrown, player ratings help influence swing decisions, contact quality, strikeouts, walks, and balls put into play.

---

The simulation evaluates every pitch individually.

Games are not resolved one at-bat at a time or from a simple outcome table.

---

Pitch quality matters.

Swing decisions matter.

Contact quality matters.

Player ratings influence all of them.

---

When a ball is put into play, ratings continue to matter.

Fielding, running, and defensive outcomes are all influenced by the players involved.

---

Players improve over time.

Participating in games earns experience points.

---

Every player has an XP bar.

As experience accumulates, that bar fills over time.

---

When the XP bar fills, the player gains a level and their ratings improve.

---

Franchises can also direct Diamonds toward player development.

Development spending helps players improve faster.

---

Because players improve over time, managers evaluate more than current ratings.

Age, experience, and development all matter.

---

The simulation engine that powers EBL is open source.

You can inspect the code, study the ratings system, and see how games are resolved.

---

You can learn more about the engine here:

Every game in EBL is built one pitch at a time using those ratings and systems.
